Prairie Lantern County: MDR-TB Community Briefing

Before you face reporters, review the background. A multidrug-resistant tuberculosis outbreak among elderly Hmong residents has exposed gaps in preparedness, language access, provider readiness, contact tracing, and trust.

Your Role

Prairie Lantern County Health Director

You must explain MDR-TB clearly, reduce stigma, protect patient privacy, support screening and follow-up, and show how local, state, community, and CDC partners are coordinating.

Escalation Timeline

How a TB case becomes a sustained public health response

Part 1 / 2015 to 2017

An index case becomes an MDR-TB outbreak

The index case dates back to 2015. By 2017, Prairie Lantern County faces a major MDR-TB outbreak among elderly Hmong residents. TB may be latent or active; MDR-TB resists at least two common TB drugs and requires prolonged treatment.

Part 2 / Community Investigation

Tracing exposure is not straightforward

The outbreak is linked to a Hmong senior activity center. Sign-in sheets are unreliable because multiple clan members share names, and the center has no formal record-keeping requirements. Stigma and historical deportation fears discourage self-identification.

Part 3 / Incident Management

The response needs structure and long-term follow-up

County public health activates ICS in 2017 to support screening events, planning, operations, finance, and liaison work. New MDR-TB cases decline, but occasional cases continue and dedicated TB capacity is built for the long term.

MDR-TB Basics

MDR-TB is active TB resistant to at least two common TB drugs. Treatment can require two years of continuous antibiotics.

Screening + Follow-Up

Screening is not a one-time event. Patients may need multiple visits, evaluation for latent or active TB, and careful treatment support.

Hmong Community Context

Many families are multigenerational, organized through clan networks, and may need trusted Hmong-speaking messengers.

Stigma + Immigration Fear

Some residents fear TB could affect immigration status or lead to community shame, making privacy and trust central.

System Readiness

Staff turnover, loss of TB expertise, limited Hmong-speaking workers, and provider confusion create response gaps.

Reporter Pressure

Reporters may ask why the county was not prepared, whether names will be released, why providers refused patients, and whether the outbreak is contained.

Before You Step To The Podium

Communication tasks for the MDR-TB briefing

  1. Explain MDR-TB in plain language without implying blame.
  2. Separate what is confirmed from what contact tracing is still determining.
  3. Protect privacy and avoid stigmatizing Hmong elders, refugees, immigrants, or families.
  4. Give concrete actions: screening, symptoms, follow-up visits, treatment support, and hotline/community contacts.
  5. Name community partners, Hmong-speaking staff, clan leaders, providers, MDH, and CDC as appropriate.
  6. Keep every answer to no more than three key messages, with no more than three supporting details each.
