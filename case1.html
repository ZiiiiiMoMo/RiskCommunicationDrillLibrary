Three-Day Escalation

How Apple Grove reaches the 1:00 PM briefing

Day 1 / Monday, May 18

Hep A(1c) exposure becomes a public action problem

MDH identifies a Hepatitis A(1c) cluster. Two adult cases are linked to workers at Rob's Roadside Restaurant, and a child case is later connected to tournament-day restaurant exposure. Officials decide a mass immune globulin clinic is urgent.

Day 2 / Thursday, May 21

The health incident becomes a multi-hazard response

A food worker's identity is exposed on social media, intensifying stigma and immigration concerns. A wildfire threatens U.S. 193 and sends residents to the hospital with respiratory distress. Firefighting demand causes unsafe water pressure and a boil water advisory.

Day 3 / Friday, May 22

The press conference becomes necessary

One elderly resident with COPD and heart disease dies from smoke inhalation. Protesters spread false claims that immune globulin is an unsafe mRNA vaccine with tracking chips. Public officials schedule a 1:00 PM press conference to reassure, update, and direct the public.

Exposure Window

People who ate at Rob's Roadside Restaurant from April 26 through May 17 may have been exposed.

Clinic Eligibility

Immune globulin is offered for people who ate there May 3 through May 16 because it works best within 14 days after exposure.

Child Risk

Hep A(1c) is described as affecting children more acutely, making parent-facing language especially important.

Wildfire Smoke

Residents, especially people with heart or lung disease, need clear advice to reduce smoke exposure and seek care for breathing trouble.

Boil Water Advisory

Residents must boil water before drinking or food preparation until testing confirms safety.

Misinformation + Protest

Reporters may ask about false claims, immigration stigma, clinic safety, privacy, trust, and whether officials acted quickly enough.

Before You Step To The Podium

Communication tasks for the briefing

  1. Lead with empathy for families, affected residents, and people worried about safety.
  2. Separate confirmed facts from what is still under investigation.
  3. Give clear actions: clinic, symptoms, hotline, smoke guidance, and boil water instructions.
  4. Protect privacy and avoid stigmatizing immigrant workers or any community.
  5. Correct misinformation plainly without repeating it more than necessary.
  6. Keep every answer to no more than three key messages, with no more than three supporting details each.
